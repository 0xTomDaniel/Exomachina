# Dagu GPL bundle review (decision-round spike)

22 September 2026. **Keep Dagu eligible for a GPL-compliant, separate-server bundle; do not treat the present spike bundle as release-ready.** This is an engineering reading of primary license sources for the tested topology, not a legal conclusion about the eventual Exomachina product.

## What the sources permit, and what the bundle actually contains

Dagu's **v2.17.0** [LICENSING.md](https://github.com/dagucloud/dagu/blob/v2.17.0/LICENSING.md) identifies its source as `GPL-3.0-or-later`. It says commercial use and distribution, including sales, are permitted **under GPL terms**. It distinguishes the separate CLI/server from importing its experimental Go API, while reserving any separate *commercial embedding* rights for a written agreement. The public GPL does not require a separate commercial license for the separate executable path when its conditions are met. These are permissions with conditions, not an unrestricted proprietary-bundle grant. The [GPLv3 text](https://github.com/dagucloud/dagu/blob/v2.17.0/LICENSE) and [GNU FAQ on selling copies](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#DoesTheGPLAllowMoney) agree that charging money is permitted.

The current [bundle builder](bundle_trial.py) copies a pinned Darwin arm64 `dagu` binary into `bin/dagu`, copies `LICENSE` and `LICENSING.md` into `licenses/dagu/`, copies Exomachina's Python adapter, launcher, Director and YAML files, and installs a managed Python plus locked Strands dependencies. The [launcher](bundle_launch.py) starts `dagu start-all` as its own process and drives it through HTTP, CLI and YAML; Exomachina does not import or link Dagu's Go package in this spike. The observed `/tmp/exomachina-decision-dagu-bundle4/inventory.json` records Dagu binary SHA-256 `fd855996bab956835043ae8cbb00ac3ea5211a6be6992374727b7908d393aac3`, license-file hashes, and Exomachina source-file names. It **does not inventory or package Dagu Corresponding Source, a source offer, or source-access directions**. The `run` wrapper starts the copied bundle; it is not itself a GPL notice or source-delivery mechanism. These facts describe a local experiment, not a released installer.

For any customer-installed distribution of that binary, GPLv3 [§4–6](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html) require preservation of appropriate copyright/license/warranty notices, a GPL license copy, and an allowed way to supply the **complete machine-readable Corresponding Source** for the precise Dagu build. Corresponding Source includes code and scripts needed to generate, install, run and modify the executable, subject to the license's exclusions. For a network-download release, §6(d) allows equivalent source access through the same download place at no further charge; a different server can host it if clear directions sit next to the binary and the distributor ensures continuing availability. Physical-product and other conveyance routes have different §6 alternatives. Copying `LICENSE` is necessary but is **not** source fulfillment. A bare upstream URL, unverified tag, or only a diff should not be assumed sufficient for this binary; the distributor must verify and preserve the source corresponding to the shipped artifact. The [GNU FAQ on redistributed binaries](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#UnchangedJustBinary) expressly says that even an unchanged third-party binary carries source obligations when redistributed.

## Separate process is useful evidence, not a safe harbor

The [GPLv3 aggregate clause in §5](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html) says putting separate, independent works on one medium does not by itself extend GPL coverage to the other works. The [GNU aggregate FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#MereAggregation) says program boundaries turn on both communication mechanism **and semantics**: sockets, pipes and command lines normally connect separate programs, but particularly intimate exchanges can make a combined work. Its [proprietary-system FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#GPLInProprietarySystem) likewise requires arms-length separation. Running `dagu start-all` beside Strands is a favorable fact compared with importing Dagu's Go API, but **separate process, one installer, or a loopback HTTP port alone does not settle whether a future product is an aggregate**. Here the bespoke Dagu DAGs call an `exo-dagu-adapter` command and the Director controls Dagu's native runs. That specific coupling, including any future embedded/custom API or packaged modifications, needs review against the *actual* product boundary. If it forms one combined covered work, licensing obligations could reach beyond the Dagu binary. If the works remain independent, Exomachina may have its own license while preserving Dagu's GPL rights.

The current spike also does not decide what Exomachina's own license, installer EULA, customer contract, supported platforms, Dagu modifications, or redistribution channel will be. GPLv3 [§10](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html) disallows extra restrictions on recipients' GPL rights; a commercial contract must not take away their right to run, modify and redistribute the Dagu component. If Dagu itself is modified, §5 adds prominent modification/date notices and GPL licensing for the modified covered work. If a future bundle is delivered in or specifically for a GPLv3 **User Product**, §6's Installation Information condition may apply. A hosted-only deployment where customers receive no copy is a different case: the [GNU FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#UnreleasedMods) says merely running a GPL program on a website does not itself require source distribution. This review is for the customer-installable path.

## Exact release gate for a Dagu bundle

1. **Freeze the artifact and boundary.** Pin each shipped Dagu platform binary and checksum to its source revision and build recipe; record whether Dagu code is modified. Inventory every Dagu-linked component and all other redistributed runtimes/dependencies. Confirm that Exomachina uses only the separate executable/server interface, and have the final process/API/DAG/adapter coupling reviewed under the aggregate-versus-combined-work test before choosing an Exomachina license/EULA.
2. **Prepare recipient rights and notices.** Keep Dagu copyright, GPL, no-warranty and applicable additional notices intact; ship the full GPL text and clear component attribution with every installer. Mark any Dagu modifications and dates. Ensure the installer, EULA, update terms and support terms do not restrict recipients' GPL rights for Dagu. Review Python/Strands and other bundled components separately.
3. **Supply Corresponding Source.** Assemble and verify the complete source **for the shipped Dagu binary**, including required build/install/run scripts and any changes, in machine-readable form. Pick and document the actual GPLv3 §6 conveyance route for each channel. For downloads, provide source beside the binary or clear equivalent-access directions beside it, at no further charge, and retain responsibility for source availability; for physical products, satisfy the applicable §6(a) or §6(b) terms. Do not count upstream hosting alone as a completed release gate.
4. **Validate the produced installer.** Inspect the final archive and download page for binary/version/hash, license/notice files, source artifact or directions, and applicable written offer. Build/rebuild or otherwise establish source-to-binary provenance without assuming byte-identical reproducibility is required. Test that a recipient can obtain and unpack the source and exercise GPL rights without product credentials or fees beyond the license's allowed terms. If the User Product rule applies, provide the required Installation Information.
5. **Recheck every release.** Repeat the boundary and artifact review after changing Dagu version, adding Dagu patches or embedded Go API use, switching installer/download channels, or changing customer contracts. Record legal review of the actual shipping package; this spike is only a feasibility screen.

The immediate engineering delta from the current bundle is concrete: add verified Dagu Corresponding Source (or a complete §6-compliant delivery path), release-facing notices/directions, and a final-package audit. None of this alone resolves the program-boundary or product-license question.
