## Product boundary and decision method

The accepted requirements are in [docs/exomachina.html](../../../../docs/exomachina.html), [docs/engine-reassessment-brief-2026-09-23.md](../../../../docs/engine-reassessment-brief-2026-09-23.md), and the [common arbitration contract](../../2026-09-22/arbitration/common/CONTRACT.md). I consulted the [candidate screen](../../../../docs/exomachina-candidate-screening.json), [selection eligibility inventory](../../../../docs/exomachina-selection-judgment.json), and the underlying results without adopting their historical ordering or numerical feature coverage as my ranking.

The customized Strands harness hosts the Factory Module and Director. Capability agents are independently managed black-box A2A services. A factory chooses approved services, orchestrates their Tasks, and accepts exact results; it does not deploy, restart, or inspect those agents' internals. A bundled local orchestration helper is acceptable. One organization per installation is the initial scope, and multiple harness instances may share a deliberately owned helper.

Factory authors must be able to compose flexible graphs from an approved vocabulary, rather than select fixed templates. Publication must preserve immutable definitions, child closure, contracts, policy, and compatible interpreter behavior for active runs. Automatic orchestration-worker rollout is allowed. Its mere occurrence is not a negative criterion.

Every candidate still needs Exomachina's semantic publisher, capability bindings, authorization, exact artifact acceptance, and remote A2A reconciliation. Native child workflows reduce local factory coordination work; they do not confer remote cancellation, discovery after a lost Task-ID reply, or exactly-once effects on arbitrary A2A peers. Product acceptance and effect ledgers are legitimate responsibilities and are not automatically second schedulers.

I favor a smaller durable recovery surface over a smaller initial source file. I also distinguish demonstrated behavior from a plausible redesign, a source finding from an injected result, and a graph-shape placeholder from an integrated product trial. No owner-approved memory ceiling exists. Resource observations inform operating cost without creating an invented pass/fail threshold.

## Why Temporal is first

The arbitration design puts authoritative acceptance in Temporal Workflow history. Native parent/child execution, waits, and recovery share that durable execution model. The Director's SQLite store supplies identity and A2A aliases; it is not another acceptance authority or scheduler. This avoids the separate-root outcome-delivery protocol required by the evaluated Dagu design and the separate acceptance-store crash boundary in Effect's parity implementation.

The [Temporal arbitration result](../../2026-09-22/arbitration/temporal/result.md) exercised separate interruptions during remote assignment, after remote Quality commit, after acceptance before release, and during a nested Director wait. Participating receivers recovered committed lost replies with one recorded effect. An opaque receiver remained durably unresolved across restart without an automatic second submission. These are consequential unattended-factory behaviors and broader evidence than Effect's current parity implementation provides.

The limits matter:

- Most post-commit crash cases began through the direct Workflow API. The additional original-Director-A2A-Task crash case interrupted assignment before remote commit. Those results do not establish the full original Task chain at every later crash point.
- The original frozen interpreter failed the hidden requirement that one immutable definition produce both success and exhaustion. The [retrospective input correction](../../2026-09-22/arbitration/temporal-input-correction/result.md) demonstrated feasibility after disclosure; it did not turn the frozen result into a blind pass.
- Replay compatibility with retained histories after that interpreter correction was not tested.
- Complete per-definition policy/service-version closure, production authorization, and active Quality-result consistency remain incomplete.

I do not award Temporal a proven Task-versus-lookup consistency advantage. Its [adapter](../../2026-09-22/arbitration/temporal/adapter.py) can return a reconciled receipt without fetching and comparing the corresponding Task payload. This is a source observation, not a new fault-injection result.

The strongest counterargument is substantial operational ownership. Exomachina would maintain the graph language and replay-compatible interpreter while packaging PostgreSQL, Temporal, workers, lifecycle management, migrations, and backups. One-command startup on a prepared host is feasible; a finished clean-machine installation is unproved. The stable interpreter permits many document versions in one worker, but interpreter code versions still require a compatibility/retention policy.

I accept that cost provisionally because the tested architecture delegates more of durable local execution and authoritative progression to one runtime. The [pinned Temporal source license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/temporalio/temporal/v1.32.0/LICENSE) is MIT. This is a favorable distribution baseline, not a completed shipped-binary and transitive-notice audit.

## Why Effect is second

The [Effect parity result](../effect-parity/result.md) materially changes its standing. A frozen interpreter executed a genuinely withheld four-branch composition with separate black-box services, a delayed branch, a typed join, routing before synthesis, and both acceptance and exhaustion from one immutable package. Native parent/child workflows retained the nested wait. A later Director integration preserved the original A2A Task across helper/Director restart and authorized abort, and recovered a participating release's lost reply.

The previous characterization as two fixed linear templates is obsolete. The implementation still supports a bounded profile: restricted assignment groups and roles, limited nesting, and a narrow parent structure. It is evidence of flexible composition within that profile, not proof of every approved graph shape the final product may need.

Effect supplies durable workflow identities, Activities, native children, concurrent execution, deferred waits, and SQLite-backed runtime state. Exomachina owns the language, validator, interpreter, publication catalog, A2A bridge, exact acceptance ledger, and lifecycle facade. Those are real maintenance obligations. The reported 1,030 runtime lines including the post-freeze Director are descriptive; they exclude a production supervisor and do not establish lower effort than other candidates.

The combination of native child coordination, SQLite operation, and [MIT source licensing](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Effect-TS/effect/main/LICENSE) gives Effect a credible simpler product path. The [earlier copied-bundle trial](../../2026-09-22/decision-round/effect/packaging-result.md) demonstrated one-command operation with two independent Directors and supervisor recovery. That packaging evidence has not been integrated and qualified with the new parity implementation, and relocation, clean-machine operation, notices, and upgrades remain open.

The strongest counterargument is evidence coverage. The parity implementation directly writes a separate product SQLite ledger around durable workflow execution. It has not passed the full in-flight, post-Quality, post-acceptance, conflicting-owner, and opaque-effect cases. Older fixed-shape trials cannot transfer those passes to this implementation. Task-versus-lookup Quality consistency remains unproved, and the stopped follow-up supplies no result. I assign no safety advantage from that absence.

My preference over Dagu is the smaller engine-specific local parent/child coordination obligation and the distribution baseline, with encouraging active-call resource evidence. Dagu remains ahead in exercised recovery coverage. Effect second is therefore an architectural product-fit judgment with a significant qualification risk, not a claim that its current implementation is more thoroughly tested.

## What the new Effect follow-ups actually establish

The [Effect in-flight report](../effect-inflight-scale/result.md) supplies a comparable held-source-assignment workload through original Director Tasks. Every held source Task was working before and after sampling. Effect retained one Python bridge per active call. It is not processless in this implementation.

The [Effect upgrade report](../effect-upgrade-stability/result.md) demonstrates that a document digest does not pin interpreter behavior. An old waiting run resumed under a replacement helper and returned the replacement's changed abort output while retaining its old document digest. Renaming the Quality Activity re-entered the three earlier review bridge calls. The stable-action lookup prevented duplicate remote submissions in this fixture; the engine Activity cache alone did not prevent re-entry after the name change.

A retained v1 helper with its own store preserved old behavior. This is useful evidence of an isolated workaround, not a production upgrade solution. The remaining proof is version-aware routing and retention across original Director Tasks, old/new helper versions, restart, in-flight work, and eventual retirement. The test used helper HTTP commands and two isolated stores. Same-store rolling deployment, dependency/schema migration, and a single process with versioned handlers were not demonstrated. Its roughly 200 MB RSS per helper is not a whole-install physical-footprint result.

The compatibility obligation also applies to Temporal's maintained interpreter. Observing an Effect upgrade hazard does not establish safety for untested Temporal upgrades. Likewise, immutable Dagu YAML does not by itself freeze the behavior of product adapter code called by that YAML.

## Why Dagu remains close, but third

Dagu supplies real native YAML graph execution and passed the original hidden same-definition composition. Its completed fault evidence is an important advantage over Effect. The [integrated bridge](../dagu-bridge-integration/result.md) also removes the earlier per-parent process retention during settled nested Director waits.

The [completed failure-recovery follow-up](../dagu-failure-recovery/result.md) closes two observed stranded-parent cases. An exhausted pre-acceptance failure becomes a sealed incident, fails the original Director Task, survives restart, and rejects accidental same-run resurrection. Separately, a gate input persisted before an enqueue error recovered through full restart and native resume; the original accepted Task completed once with its original identities and one effect. The enqueue injection reached the observable durable gap; it did not instrument a kill within the engine's exact instruction-level window.

The maintained protocol is my concern. The product coordinates independent roots, retry accounting, failure sealing, parent-gate delivery, and continuation recovery. The demonstrated failure rule covers one pre-acceptance failed step after two completed product retries. A crash during the second retry can leave the completion marker unset and prevent sealing. Longer queue outages, post-acceptance incidents, and unknown external effects need further policies. The implementation also needs generalization beyond its named fixture steps.

The [original Dagu arbitration result](../../2026-09-22/arbitration/dagu/result.md) records additional costs: explicit conditional-descendant router wiring, a demonstrated frozen Quality identity-guard failure, and a participating-release liveness gap when a recorded send never reached the receiver. These are not erased by the newer parent-recovery fixes. Conversely, the Quality guard failure is not proof of a live network exploit or an irreparable engine defect.

The [active-assignment follow-up](../dagu-inflight-scale/result.md) shows that processless parent waits do not make the current synchronous assignment path processless. A gate/scanner adaptation appears feasible, but would add maintained remote submission, Task discovery, timeout/cancellation, authority, and persisted-input recovery rules. That adaptation has not been implemented or fault-tested.

Dagu remains eligible for a compliant separate-server distribution. Its [v2.17.0 licensing guidance](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dagucloud/dagu/v2.17.0/LICENSING.md) permits commercial activity under GPL terms and distinguishes a separate executable from the embedded Go API. The [actual bundle review](../../2026-09-22/decision-round/dagu/gpl-bundle-review.md) found no completed Corresponding Source delivery arrangement. The final program boundary and recipient rights also remain to be reviewed. Separate processes are relevant evidence, not automatic clearance. GPL is a distribution obligation here, not a categorical exclusion.

The strongest valid argument for Dagu second is that it has a native authoring layer and more completed recovery evidence, including the new original-Task incident and enqueue recovery. My contrary judgment weights the ongoing independent-root protocol and distribution obligations more heavily. This is the central disagreement, rather than a dispute over whether Dagu's passing results count.

## Temporal through Zigflow

The [Zigflow reassessment](../zigflow-reassessment/result.md) makes it a serious alternative authoring route within Temporal. Automatic worker rollout is allowed, and three retained versions preserved old waits while leaving an independent A2A service untouched. This removes the obsolete no-worker-refresh objection.

The graphs only waited for signals and set values. Zigflow did not perform product-shaped A2A assignment, Quality, exact acceptance, repair, or nested-factory behavior in this trial. An immediate start after successful activation actually selected the previous version; guarded canaries subsequently passed. A product publication barrier or explicit version route, plus safe retention and retirement, remains owned work.

Zigflow can remove much of the custom generic interpreter, but not Exomachina's semantic publisher, safe primitive profile, capability closure, A2A lifecycle, authorization, or rollout machinery. The observed 303/360/405 MiB for one/two/three retained versions covers the engine and excludes the agent bundle. I do not transfer Temporal SDK features or the Python adapter's fault results into the Zigflow DSL. The more complete stable-interpreter route is my first choice today; Zigflow remains an option that could reduce future authoring ownership if its integrated contract passes.
