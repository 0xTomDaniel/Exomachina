# Kestra OSS parity audit and bounded fresh graph trial

23 September 2026. **Kestra deserves a matched finalist comparison before its third-place rank is treated as settled.** The 22 September [arbitration contract](../../2026-09-22/arbitration/common/CONTRACT.md) explicitly prioritized Dagu and Temporal, admitting Kestra and Effect as conditional comparators if the finalists failed or product-owned work remained unclear. Temporal's frozen A2 same-definition case failed, and the exact ownership cost of its product-owned interpreter remains unclear. Earlier Kestra S0–S4 evidence was substantial, but it was not a common A1–A5 arbitration run. The later [judgment](../../../../docs/engine-arbitration-2026-09-22.md) ranks Kestra third and Effect fourth without that parity evidence. This is a legitimate provisional judgment, not an observed third-versus-fourth or first-versus-third result.

## Prior evidence and the decisive gap

- [S0](../../2026-09-22/s0/kestra/result.md) ran native Kestra OSS v2.0.3 with JDK 25 and PostgreSQL 16.15, preserving a paused execution across full process restart. The [S0 local bundle](../../2026-09-22/s0/kestra/package/result.md) started copied JDK, Kestra and PostgreSQL binaries with one command on one Mac. Its shaded `isorelax` license/NOTICE uncertainty and clean-machine distribution remained open. The ~650 MiB RSS observation was the JVM alone, not a whole factory installation.
- [S3](../../2026-09-22/s3/result.md) showed native flow revisions, explicit child revision pinning, parallel children, one repair, and a Director Pause without refreshing a worker. It also showed that an unpinned parent can use a new child and native Kestra validation/publishing accepts a review-bypass flow. Its Quality and release tasks were placeholders.
- [S2](../../2026-09-22/s2/kestra-result.md) and [S4](../../2026-09-22/s4/result.md) separately exercised a Strands/A2A factory endpoint, one native Pause, exact artifact acceptance and selected lost-acknowledgement gaps. S2's owned `KestraFactory` implementation explicitly assumes one Pause and its `_refresh` maps only a directly paused execution to an input-required A2A Task. S4's combined case had an immediately completed A2A task; it did not inject a lost A2A reply before Kestra recorded it.

The missing discriminator is one frozen, integrated nested factory run through independently managed black-box A2A agents, Quality, exact acceptance/release, negative publication/authority cases, autonomous fault recovery and a Director-routed 0/2/10 wait footprint. Neither S3 placeholders nor this new bounded trial supplies that proof. The [feature matrix](../../../../docs/exomachina-feature-matrix.json) is documented capability coverage; it does not resolve these owned-work and operating questions.

## Frozen native authoring trial

The [generic native API driver](api.py) and [isolated launcher](start.sh) were frozen at **2026-09-23 13:28:53 UTC**. The evaluator received [inventory SHA-256 `2fbd96a9dff2facdd57926d14bcfe7d4c7d0d2b5842c8e6cc46e0b5df65c3733`](evidence/freeze.json) before revealing a fresh four-branch A2B arrangement. `freeze.py verify` passed after the new definitions and all runs. The engine was the pinned Kestra OSS v2.0.3 executable, SHA-256 `b4b4518a617f93965dcf98c9673beda3dad2b6d9d1780ad8b9a945e437975ab4`; PostgreSQL 16.15 and JDK 25 were the copied S0 bundle. Only [native YAML definitions](definitions/) and probe/measurement drivers were authored after the freeze. There was no Kestra server or worker code rollout across v1–v4 publication. Automatic supervised worker rollout, if a future authoring path needs it, is acceptable under the clarified product requirement if active Task/version identities remain exact; the observed native path did not need one.

The old [v1 parent](evidence/start-parent-v1.json) started its pinned v1 child, which reached native `PAUSED`. New v2, v3a and v3b child and parent YAML published as native revisions 2, 3 and 4 while that v1 run waited. The [fresh v3a child](definitions/child-v3a.yaml) starts four named parallel branches: two source instances and two counter instances. The counter-primary branch sleeps two seconds. In its [success execution](evidence/child-v3a-success.json), the other three finished at approximately 13:31:36.97–36.98 UTC, counter-primary finished at 13:31:39.075, and `join_four` started at 13:31:39.134. The [join output](evidence/join-v3a-success.json) retained all four distinct static receipt IDs and digest strings. The route ran after join and before synthesis. Counter-primary said `clear`, counter-crosscheck said `requires_scope`, and the native [route result](evidence/route-bool-v3a-success.json) was `true`.

**One immutable revision 3** handled both run inputs: the [one-repair case](evidence/child-v3a-success.json) reached placeholder `quality_r2_positive`, acceptance and release; the [two-repair case](evidence/child-v3a-exhausted.json) reached placeholder negative reviews through r3 and a nested child `PAUSED` state while its parent remained `RUNNING`. The [v3b definition](definitions/child-v3b.yaml), native revision 4, changed the second counter to `clear`; its [route result](evidence/route-bool-v3b-direct.json) was `false`, and its [execution](evidence/child-v3b-direct.json) took the direct r1 placeholder Quality/acceptance/release path. The parent used a pinned native `Subflow` with `wait: true` in all versions.

Stopping and restarting **both Kestra and PostgreSQL** preserved the [old child at revision 1 and `PAUSED`](evidence/restart-old-child.json), its [parent at revision 1 and `RUNNING`](evidence/restart-old-parent.json), the [v3a exhausted child at revision 3 and `PAUSED`](evidence/restart-v3a-child-wait.json), and its [parent at revision 3 and `RUNNING`](evidence/restart-v3a-parent-wait.json). After manual native resume, the old parent [returned `child-v1`](evidence/old-parent-output.json), and the v3a exhausted parent [completed](evidence/after-abort-parent-v3a.json). This is native revision/wait persistence. The manual resume lacked a product Director authority check, and the placeholder abort path ended native `SUCCESS`, so it is **not** a valid A1 authorized-abort result.

**A2B classification: partial.** Kestra natively executed the newly revealed four-way graph, delayed join, route-before-synthesis, same-definition repair/exhaustion, direct path and old-version retention without executor changes. The static `Return` branches did not contact the independently managed A2A agents; their printed digest strings were not recomputed or checked against actual artifacts; and the join did not enforce declared branch result types. The route `If` produced a Boolean evaluation result, but the child did not materialize a separately validated typed `join.requires_scope` field. Placeholder Quality, acceptance and release tasks cannot establish independent identity, authoritative state, remote effects or their failure recovery. Native schema validation of these YAML files is not Exomachina's safety publisher.

## Whole-process footprint, with route limits

The [measurement driver](measure.py) included the copied Kestra/JDK and PostgreSQL processes, two real Strands capability harnesses, independent Quality harness, participating and opaque release receivers, an S2 Kestra-configured Strands Director process, and the measurement process itself. The Director process was **idle**; nested parent/child waits were admitted directly through the native API because S2's one-Pause Director implementation cannot govern this graph unchanged. These are not matched Director-routed A1–A5 workloads. The same Darwin `footprint -f bytes --noCategories` method as the [Dagu/Temporal wait probe](../../2026-09-22/arbitration/wait-scaling/result.md) measured one de-duplicated process set, including the measurement driver and its transient `ps` child.

| Nested waits | De-duplicated physical footprint | Selected PIDs |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 1,284,601,704 B | 35 |
| 2 | 1,287,387,008 B | 35 |
| 10 | 1,301,002,448 B | 36 |

The [raw process lists and stage identities](evidence/footprint.json) show no per-wait process growth in this native Subflow/Pause route; the 0-to-10 measured increase was about 16.4 MB. All ten child waits reached `PAUSED`; their parents remained pending, and all ten [completed after manual resumes](evidence/footprint-completed.json). The one-PID difference was a PostgreSQL process variation; both stages also include a transient `ps` child. The bundle's copied runtime was 418 MiB, copied PostgreSQL 125 MiB, and this trial's initialized state about 73 MiB (`du` on this host), roughly 616 MiB together. This is host-local disk, not an installer size or redistribution clearance.

For orientation only, the earlier **Director-routed** Dagu/Temporal arbitration measured 548/715/1,303 MB and 757/791/849 MB respectively at 0/2/10 waits. Kestra's low incremental wait cost is promising, while its high idle baseline remains a substantial operating cost. The services were idle in this Kestra run and the state had completed trial executions before the 0-wait snapshot; the routes and workloads differ. No owner-approved memory envelope or clean-machine supported-platform result exists, so these figures cannot settle a ranking.

## Independent selection judgment

Kestra's third-place rationale over Effect has a concrete basis: Kestra supplies a native document graph, versioned publication, pinned Subflow execution and processless nested waits. Effect's tested fixed-shape interpreter did not supply flexible governed composition, even though Effect showed stronger local crash recovery and a lighter SQLite/Node topology. That is an ownership tradeoff, not a completed common-score comparison. Kestra's Apache-2.0 core and local bundle are eligible for development testing; its exact redistribution/NOTICE, shared-helper authorization, lifecycle and resource envelope remain conditional.

This trial **does not displace Temporal or Dagu**. It does show that treating Kestra's graph authoring as a mere historical S3 footnote would miss a real advantage over product-owned interpreters. Before finalizing Temporal-specific packaging, run Kestra through the same integrated A1–A5 contract with black-box A2A services, a restricted publisher and complete closure, real Quality identity and exact acceptance/release, authorized nested abort, autonomous lost-acknowledgement recovery, and a Director-routed shared-install footprint. The inability of the existing S2 Director Module to carry this graph is a concrete product integration gap, not a Kestra engine failure. A consistent resource/operating threshold is needed to decide whether Kestra's large idle baseline is acceptable.
