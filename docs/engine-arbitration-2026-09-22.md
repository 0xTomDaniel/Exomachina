The [fresh reassessment](engine-reassessment-2026-09-23.md) ranks Temporal's stable interpreter first, Effect second, and Dagu third for development after new frozen composition, recovery, rollout, and held-call evidence. The observations below remain part of the record; no candidate is production-qualified.

22 September 2026. An adversarial review by GPT-6 Astra xhigh and Claude Opus 5.5 high considered all nine still-eligible candidates, challenged each other's rankings, and converged on **Temporal first, Dagu second** for Exomachina's first product development. This is a moderate-confidence direction for development, not production qualification or a claim that Temporal currently passes every factory contract. Their judgment was holistic: authored graph flexibility, ownership of durable nested execution, the one-install footprint, integration and recovery code, rights, and remaining uncertainty mattered alongside feature scores. The independent agents initially disagreed on the order and changed their positions after additional frozen trials; this order was not supplied to them.

## Evidence that changed the ranking

Both candidates ran a common product-shaped Strands/A2A 0.3.0 fixture with independent capability, Quality, Director, and release processes. The [contract](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/common/CONTRACT.md), [freeze receipts](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/common/freeze-receipts.json), and [hidden A2 reveal](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/common/HIDDEN_A2_REVEAL.md) define the tests and show frozen implementation inventories before the withheld composition. These fixtures test execution and authority boundaries, not model quality or A2A 1.x compatibility.

Dagu's [arbitration result](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/dagu/result.md) passed the hidden three-branch composition under one immutable `requires_scope` definition: it produced both one-repair success and two-repair exhaustion, plus a clear-route direct acceptance. The frozen Temporal [arbitration result](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/temporal/result.md) expressed the branches, typed join, route, repair, and old-version retention, but **failed the same-definition outcome condition**: success and exhaustion required distinct immutable packages. That is a real interpreter/definition-input defect to fix, not a passing A2 result. Both candidates also have incomplete per-definition service/Quality-policy closure and partial authority/recovery coverage.

The matched [Director-routed wait trial](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/wait-scaling/result.md) measured complete trial bundles at 0/2/10 waiting parent/child pairs. Dagu used **548/715/1,303 MB** and **9/15/39 PIDs**; Temporal used **757/791/849 MB** and **49 PIDs** throughout. Dagu's frozen parent path retained three more processes per pair. These are point-in-time measurements on one Mac, not a production budget or a linear scaling law. Temporal's memory rose about 93 MB across ten waits; only its process count stayed constant.

A separate [Dagu processless-wait trial](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/dagu-wait-alternative/result.md) showed that native parent and child human tasks plus a single shared scanner can preserve native run IDs and recover certain restart and lost-ack cases with no per-wait process. Its 104 MB ten-pair figure excludes the full A2A/Quality/release bundle and cannot be compared to the complete-bundle numbers. A child-final-step callback with step retries was stranded by an injected kill; that does **not** prove all native Dagu retry configurations fail. The shared scanner worked in its bounded cases, but it has not yet carried the original factory A2A Task, accepted **and** aborted outcomes, full artifact authority, or the input-persisted-before-enqueue failure boundary.

Temporal is preferred because it supplies native durable child execution, Workflow history for acceptance, and bounded engine/PostgreSQL restart recovery without an additional cross-root completion protocol. It has a heavier idle installation, a product-owned interpreter/validator, replay compatibility obligations, and bundled PostgreSQL lifecycle. Dagu retains real advantages in native YAML authoring, lower idle use, faster observed startup, and its successful frozen hidden-composition test. Its current product path also has a [Quality identity-guard failure](../tools/spikes/2026-09-22/arbitration/dagu/a3-identity-gap.json) and a participating-release liveness gap; neither is evidence that the engine itself is unsuitable. Temporal's forged/stale Quality-result checks were not injected into the active Activity, and its A2A-origin crash was before the remote commit; post-commit crashes used direct Workflow starts. These limits remain material.

## Development and release consequences

Begin Temporal development by binding outcome-affecting values from each run and repeating the hidden same-definition composition under a fresh freeze. Replay retained histories after interpreter changes. Test forged and stale results through the active Quality Activity, and exercise post-commit crash/ack boundaries through the original Director A2A Task. Proposed fixes do not count as observed passes.

Before product release, prove complete version closure for service and policy revisions, authorization on direct engine/API and Update paths, clean-machine one-command startup, backup/restore and supported upgrade with waits in flight, history and budget limits, the assembled distribution/notice audit, and the scoped A2A protocol version. Optional autoresearch and A2A 1.x become release gates only if included in the shipped scope.

An integrated Dagu processless bridge is the one plausible order-reversing countertrial. It should carry both accepted and authorized-abort outcomes through the original A2A Task, cover the persisted-input/enqueue boundary and child failure, and remeasure the **complete** bundle at 0/2/10 waits. Both reviewers agree it is not necessary before choosing a development direction. Opus recommends it before committing to Temporal-specific packaging; Astra would start Temporal development without delaying for it. No absolute memory threshold or projected integrated-Dagu footprint has been established.
